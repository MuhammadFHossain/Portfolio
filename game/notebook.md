# Game notebook

A running record of the game's story. Three lists: decided, open and parked.
Only the author moves something to decided.

## How we work

- One small question at a time. Half an answer is fine, and so is "I don't know yet."
- The author's words first. Claude says back what it heard before adding anything.
- Claude asks before offering ideas, and offers one or two plain ones.
- When the author says "maybe", it is a brainstorm: Claude joins in with plain
  ideas and an honest opinion. Nothing from a brainstorm is decided until the
  author says so.
- When something feels off, we stop there.
- Scenes before plot.
- No building until the author says so.

## Decided

Quotes are the author's own words.

**What the player should come away with** (never said in the game)

- We only see a slice of each other's lives. "Parents don't see the end of my
  life, spouse doesn't see the start of it, we see slivers."
- Honour: "see the evil but still choose to do good, regardless of if it is
  rewarded."

**The feeling of the ending**

- Frodo returning to the Shire: "nobody knows what just happened, and nobody
  will really understand."
- "The return gets you back to humility and base."
- "The sacrifice at the end can't be her dying too, or even him." The brother's
  is the only death of the three.

**The game**

- A fantasy open world, beautiful, "flowers fluttering", kind of inspired by
  Ghost of Tsushima. Made for iPhone.
- "A game of journey, meeting new friends, seeing a sliver, and somehow getting
  to see more than another would, and in the end coming home."
- It starts with "something by accident".
- Not built like Lord of the Rings.
- Show, don't tell. "The whole game has to be like that."
- For everyone, made with adults in mind. (Claude's reading; not yet confirmed.)

**The kid**

- "Timid."
- "Not shy per se but just not brave, but he actually is. Brave is when there
  is something to be brave about."
- His bravery is for family or a friend. "It just comes out, and he doesn't
  even realize it." Like Sam.
- At the end: "confident but humble and honourable, kind and gentle and
  generous, standing tall but still not brash at all, mild and mannered and
  elegant."
- He is not a Strider type who becomes a king.
- After his brother dies, what he wants most is "to bring him back. He can't
  imagine life without him. He has no idea what to do."

**The older brother**

- "A joker, fun, charismatic."
- "The better one": brave and much stronger. "You think he is the main
  character and maybe you play as him."
- The player plays as him, and for a long time: "it should be long, you should
  think you are playing as the brother for the game."
- As him, "you should build some abilities too, get stronger too, feel like
  you will be him."
- He dies, early. The kid has to "pick up the slack". "It's a devastating
  thing."
- He "dies trying to save both his brother and his mother (through the item
  that will save her)."
- What kills him is "the world, something by accident". Nobody is to blame.
  There is no revenge arc: "the revenge arc would suck, and becomes closer to
  Big Hero 6."
- How: he holds something so the kid can get through, "with him trying but it
  just doesn't work. It's almost hopeful it will, but it's just not enough."
- His death is not left in doubt: "he does die, it's clear, or it's basically
  understood there's no way."
- The kid is swept away too, far, and is lost. He survived "because of a
  sacrifice of the brother".

**Right after the brother dies**

- "Even the person who's playing is a little scared, like, what just happened?
  What did I do? It's a shocking moment."
- Then the game lets them "breathe and try to pick up the pieces and figure out
  what to do. Slow it down all the way for a bit."
- When it is quiet: "he is cold, there is water and debris, he is scared,
  dirty." The thing is "in his satchel or something". "Even he doesn't know
  where he is."

**The mother**

- A mother is waiting at home. "She is very sick. He can't lose them both, and
  the player can't allow that."
- The brothers' trip "was to get something to use to save her."
- When his brother dies, the kid "has it and has to carry it home."

**The orphan**

- Later the kid finds a younger orphan "to take care of and elevate, and finds
  himself on the way."

**Gifts**

- Gifts, "kind of like JoJo's".
- They are "powers that they have to uncover inside". They are not things
  handed over by other people.
- Not overdone.
- The player doesn't throw powers around. "They kind of have to give it all up
  to complete it, and it sucks but it's the right thing." Like Aunt May's
  sacrifice in Spider-Man.
- Losing them is fine. "It could even help them close and end the game."

**The world**

- People are stingy. "Just how they are."
- There is a famine.

## Open

1. What "give it all up" means for powers that are inside a person, or
   borrowed.
2. What the pieces are: what is around the kid, and what he does, in the slow
   stretch after the storm.
3. What the sacrifice at the end is, and what it is for. It is not a death.
4. What happens to the orphan.
5. How the game keeps it secret, inside and outside the game, that the brother
   is not the hero.
6. With no villain, what stands in his way on the road home.
7. Who the friends on the road are.
8. What the player does from moment to moment.
9. The look of the world, and names.
10. Who has powers, and how one gets uncovered. Whether the brother's rope is
    a skill anyone could learn, or his power.
11. Where "there" is, and what the something that can save her is.
12. How the player can feel "what did I do?" without thinking they failed and
    trying again.
## Parked

Ideas that are not decided. Most came from Claude.

- Powers are "really random", the JoJo way. The author hasn't seen that in
  games. (Author's aside.)
- "Maybe the main character never has it, and needs to borrow somehow, kind of
  like Sokka maybe, or All Might style." (Author's aside.)
- "Maybe the point of the game was to get there and it wasn't so hard, and now
  he is stuck there and has to make it back." Like the Spider-Verse scene where
  the older Spider-Man saves Miles and Miles still has to escape. (Author's
  maybe. That he carries the thing home is decided.)
- "Maybe they are poor." (Brainstorm.)
- The accident: "kind of like Man of Steel tornado maybe, but people hate
  that." (Brainstorm.) Keep the picture: a huge storm, one brother in it, the
  other unable to reach him. Avoid what people hated: a death the watcher could
  have stopped, and a reason too small for the cost.
- The brother's ability (brainstorm): "some sort of sly, cheeky, maybe chain
  swords, but I don't want it too close to God of War, maybe like Scorpion
  style fighting ability, swing with it to carry the brother, make bridges
  with it." Claude's take: one rope or chain with a hook, a tool before it is
  a weapon, which keeps it away from God of War. The bridge he holds in the
  storm is the same move the player has made all along. Without it, every gap
  he crossed in a second is a problem for the kid.
- The first thing he does after the storm is check the satchel. (Claude's
  idea.)
- While playing the brother, the player looks after the kid. Later, as the
  kid, the player looks after the orphan. (Claude's noticing.)
- Around the brother's death (brainstorm). The kid is already carrying the
  item, so saving the kid saves both. The player does the holding as the
  brother, then becomes the kid. He jokes to keep the kid calm, right to the
  end.
- A loop: the ending starts someone else's journey. (The author floated "a loop
  sort of thing to start again"; its shape is undecided.)
- The kid teaches the orphan what his brother taught him.
- The kid froze when his brother died and thinks he is a coward.
- His journal leaves himself out. Strangers tell his story and he doesn't
  recognise it.
- Help left on the road for other real players.
- Engine: Unity 6, built on the author's Mac. First build: one meadow.
- Size: a small, dense world instead of a huge one.

## Dropped

- Flower fields as memorials, the two bedrolls, "every gift has a giver".
  Too forced.
- Friends on the road each handing him a gift.
- Both brothers being orphans. A mother waiting at home felt right.
- The trip being for food. It is to get something to save their mother.
- A person causing the brother's death, and any revenge arc.
- Earlier directions: Slivers (a life seen through windows), The Night Ferry,
  The Courier, the guardian's oath.
